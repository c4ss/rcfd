**Reading (registered here).**

1. "Candidate cell": a cell of the other part whose weight in the fringe cell's interpolant can be non-zero at some pose, that
   is, a cell within its support radius (D2 below). "Changes kind": between calculated and fringe or hole.
2. "Continuous across every face of a uniform mesh": the interpolant, as a function of the point in the donor part, has no jump
   where the point crosses a face of the donor mesh; measured by the jump estimator of A3 at a point of every internal face.
3. "The linear stencil jumps by at least 1e-4": the library's `Linear` stencil (the donor and its calculated face neighbours,
   ADR 0219 M4) gives, at the same point, a jump of at least `1e-4` by the same estimator. The clause is the negative control
   that the test field and the points make the jump visible; it is registered as a count (A3).
4. TG_2, `d_N`, `e(TG_2)`: ADR 0359 reading 1 (BDF2, three outer iterations, `rhie_chow_step_consistent`, 200, 400 and 800
   steps to `t = 0.5`); "the component cells" are the composite cells `n_background_cells..` that are not holes (all of them;
   the component has no hole). The ratio is `max abs(d_200) / max abs(d_400)` over the component cells. The value of the linear
   stencil is ADR 0359's S0 run, `e = 3.1859e-4` (printed as `3.186e-4`); it is not run again (the defaults are shown unchanged
   by F0).

## Design (D1 to D9)

**D1. The variant.** `DonorInterpolation::Continuous`, set by `Overset::set_donor_interpolation` before the solver is built,
like `Quadratic`. `Linear` stays the default; nothing of the `Linear` or `Quadratic` code path is changed.

**D2. The candidates.** For a fringe cell, `x` is its centre in the reference frame of the other part (as in M3: the moved
centre for a component fringe cell, `pose_inverse_apply` of the centre for a background fringe cell). For every cell `k` of
the other part, `x_k` is its reference centre and `h_k` the largest extent, over the solved directions, of the bounding box
of its reference points; `rho_k = |x - x_k| / h_k`, the distance taken in the solved directions. The candidates are the
**calculated** cells `k` of the other part with `rho_k < kappa`, `kappa = 1.5`. The reference mesh never changes, so `x_k` and
`h_k` do not depend on the pose; the donor cell is not used to choose them.

**D3. The weight.** `W_k(x) = (1 - (rho_k / kappa)^2)^2 / (rho_k^2 + eps^2)` for `rho_k < kappa`, zero otherwise, with
`eps = 0.5`. The first factor is a compactly supported bump: it and its derivative vanish at `rho_k = kappa`, so `W_k` is
continuously differentiable in `x` everywhere (also where `k` enters or leaves the candidates). The second factor is the
library's own least-squares weight (`1 / (|r|^2 + small)`, M4) with a core `eps`, which pulls the fit towards the nearest
centres (a smaller interpolation error, the arithmetic below) while staying bounded.

**D4. The weights of the stencil (moving least squares, linear basis).** With `r_k = x_k - x` in the solved directions and the
length `L = (sum_k W_k |r_k|^2 / sum_k W_k)^(1/2)` (a continuous function of `x`), `p_k = (1, r_k / L)`. The weights are the
minimiser of `sum_k w_k^2 / W_k` under `sum_k w_k p_k = e_0` (`e_0 = (1, 0, ..., 0)`). Derivation (Lagrange multipliers
`lambda`): `w_k / W_k = p_k . lambda`, so `w_k = W_k p_k . lambda` and the constraint gives `M lambda = e_0` with `M = sum_k
W_k p_k p_k^T`. These are the weights with which the weighted least-squares plane through the candidate values, `min sum_k
W_k (a . p_k - u_k)^2`, gives its value `a_0` at `x` (`a = M^{-1} sum W_k p_k u_k`, `a_0 = e_0 . a = sum_k (W_k p_k^T M^{-1}
e_0) u_k`, `M` symmetric): the moving least squares of Lancaster and Salkauskas with a linear basis. **Linear exactness**: for
`u = c + g . (y - x)`, `u_k = (c, L g) . p_k`, so `sum w_k u_k = (c, L g) . sum w_k p_k = (c, L g) . e_0 = c = u(x)`.
`sum_k w_k = 1` is the first row of the constraint. The scaling by `L` changes nothing in exact arithmetic (the fit is
invariant under an affine change of the basis), only the round-off. `M` is solved by the elimination with partial pivoting of
`linear_weights` (the same pivot test, `1e-12` of the largest entry), in a new function; `linear_weights` is not touched.

**D5. Continuity in the pose (the argument the checks test).** (i) The fringe point `x(pose)` is a continuous function of the
pose (a rigid motion and its inverse applied to a fixed point; the moved centre of a component cell is the pose applied to its
reference centre up to round-off). (ii) For fixed kinds, sum over **every** calculated cell of the other part:
`M(x) = sum_k W_k(x) p_k p_k^T` and `w_k(x) = W_k(x) p_k^T M(x)^{-1} e_0`; the cells outside the support have `W_k = 0` and
add nothing, so the sum over the candidates equals the sum over all cells. Each `W_k` is continuous (D3), `L` is continuous
and positive, so `M` is continuous, and where `M` is invertible `M^{-1}` and every `w_k` are continuous; a cell that enters or
leaves the candidates does so with `w_k = 0`. Hence the fringe value `sum_k w_k u_k` is a continuous (indeed continuously
differentiable) function of `x` and of the pose wherever no candidate changes kind and `M` stays invertible. (iii) The donor
cell does not enter the values. Where continuity is lost: a candidate changing kind (outside the acceptance), and the fallback
of D6 (counted, required to be zero on every checked mesh). The library's linear stencil is chosen by the donor and jumps when
`x` crosses a face; the bilinear B of ADR 0359 is continuous with kinks (its derivative jumps at the lines between centres) and
defined only on uniform rectangular meshes; this one is defined on any mesh.

**D6. Fallback.** Fewer candidates than solved directions plus one, a degenerate `M` (D4's pivot test), or `sum |w_k|` above
`4` (`QUADRATIC_MAX_ABS_SUM` of ADR 0251 R3, the bound on a one-sided stencil, reused with its reason): the fringe cell gets the
library's linear stencil and is listed in `Connectivity::fallbacks`, as with `Quadratic` (ADR 0251 D4). The "no donor"
refusal of M3 (the point in no cell of the other part, or its donor not calculated) is kept unchanged.

**D7. The candidate search.** For each part, once, when `Continuous` is set: uniform bins over the union of the support boxes
(`x_k +- kappa h_k` in the solved directions, the cell's box in the others), each cell in every bin its support box overlaps
(bin size as in `CellLocator`: the cube root of the box volume per cell). A query at `x` reads the bin that contains `x` and
keeps the calculated cells with `rho_k < kappa`. Every cell whose support contains `x` is in that bin (its support box
contains `x`), so the search is exact; A6 checks it against a search over every cell.

**D8. The stencil.** The candidates in increasing cell order (not the donor first: the order of the sum then changes only when
a cell enters or leaves, with a zero weight) and their weights. The documentation of `Stencil` says so for `Continuous`.

**D9. The solver.** One condition in `crates/rcfd-fv/src/overset.rs`: the refusal of a non-positive interpolated rAU (ADR 0251
D5) is made for every interpolation other than `Linear` (the moving least-squares weights are not guaranteed non-negative on
an irregular mesh). With `Linear` the condition is false as before: the default path is unchanged.

## Arithmetic before this registration (disclosed)

By the Python scripts named above (model grids of unit spacing, numpy, nothing of the solver):

- **The support radius.** On a uniform grid of spacing `h` the point farthest from the centres off a line is a face midpoint
  plane: the two cells of the face lie on the normal through it (`0.5 h`), and the plane through it needs the four cells at
  `sqrt(1.25) h = 1.118 h`. For those to carry a weight of at least about a fifth of the nearest pair's bump, `1.118 / kappa <=
  0.75`: `kappa = 1.5` (bump `0.197` against `0.79`). At the dual-cell centre the four cells at `0.707 h` suffice.
- **The core `eps`**, on the unit grid, field `u = (x^2 + y^2) / 2` (Hessian the identity), 1681 points of a dual cell; error
  in units of `h^2 |D^2 u|`:

| Interpolant | max error | mean error | largest `sum abs(w)` | smallest weight |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| library linear (donor and 4 face neighbours) | 0.210 | 0.163 | - | - |
| bilinear B | 0.250 | 0.169 | 1 | 0 |
| D3 with `kappa = 1.5`, no core (bump alone) | 0.298 | 0.277 | 1.000 | 0 |
| D3, `kappa = 1.5`, `eps = 1.0` | 0.250 | 0.229 | 1.000 | 0 |
| **D3, `kappa = 1.5`, `eps = 0.5`** | **0.250** | **0.195** | **1.000** | **3.8e-7** |
| D3, `kappa = 1.5`, `eps = 0.3` | 0.250 | 0.180 | 1.011 | -5.3e-3 |
| D3, `kappa = 1.3`, `eps = 0.5` | 0.250 | 0.173 | 1.001 | -2e-4 |

  `eps = 0.5` is the smallest of the cores tried that keeps every weight positive on the uniform grid (the condition number of
  `M` at most 2.7) with an error 1.15 times that of B on average and B's maximum. On grids of aspect 2 and 3 (`h_k` the larger
  extent) and on a 3-D unit grid the weights stay positive (`sum abs(w) = 1.0000`, condition at most 4.7), 4 to 9 candidates in
  2-D and 8 to 19 in 3-D.
- **The jump of the linear stencil across a face** (`model3.py`): for `u = x^2 + 3 x y - y^2` on the grid of TG_2's background
  (`h = pi / 80`), at the face centre the two linear stencils agree by symmetry (jump `1e-19`); at the point `x_f + 0.25 h t`
  (`t` along the face) the jump is `1.157e-3` across faces of both orientations (the Hessian is constant, so the same at every
  interior face). So A3 uses that point and that field: the linear jump is ten times its `1e-4` threshold.
- **The jump estimator.** With `D(delta) = I(x + delta n) - I(x - delta n)`, `J = 2 D(delta) - D(2 delta)`. For a continuously
  differentiable `I`, `D(delta) = 2 delta I' + O(delta^3)` and `J = O(delta^3)`; for an interpolant with a jump `j` at `x`,
  `J = j + O(delta)`. With `delta = 1e-6 h` the cubic term is about `1e-19` and the round-off about `4 * 2.2e-16 * max|u|`
  (`3e-14` on TG_2, `2e-12` on TR, whose coordinates reach 25): both far below `1e-9`. `delta = 1e-6 h` is far above the
  locator's tolerance (`1e-10` of the cell diagonal), so the two sides have different donors.
- **The flow (estimate, a prediction only).** ADR 0359 H4: B on the component fringe alone takes the component cells to
  `1.158e-6`, ratio `4.146`; B on the background fringe alone leaves them at `2.06e-5` (the background fringe's switches do not
  reach the component cells). D3 is continuously differentiable in the pose where B has kinks, so I expect the component cells
  at about `1.2e-6` with a ratio of about 4.2 (B everywhere gave `1.13e-6` over the far cells, ratio `3.69`, a maximum that may
  sit in a background cell). `e(TG_2)`: B lowered it from `3.186e-4` to `2.841e-4` with an error equal to the library's on
  average; D3's error is 1.2 times larger on average: I expect about `2.9e-4`, with the risk of a miss stated here.
- Cost: TG_2 with up to 9 cells per stencil in place of 5; three runs about 7 minutes (ADR 0359: 6).

## Decision rule

- **done**: A1, A1b, A2a, A2b, A3, A3b, A4, A5, A6, A7, A8 all met; M1 to M5 each caught by its registered check.
- **partial**: the variant is built, A1, A1b, A2a and A2b hold, and some other check is missed or not run, or a mutation is not
  caught by its registered check (the ADR then names why, with the number, as the acceptance allows).
- **todo**: no variant is built, or a default changed (A1 or A1b missed).

## Open choices taken

Each taken on the maintainer's standing instruction of 2026-11-11 (take the recommendation); none is a decision the maintainer
made in person; none changes a default, a golden or a hash table.

- **O1. Take ADR 0359 question 1 now** (recommendation (a)): taken, as the note of F-468 says.
- **O2. Which continuous interpolant.** (a) moving least squares with a linear basis and per-cell supports (D2 to D4); (b) the
  same with a quadratic basis (more exact, but a support of about `2 h` and more fallbacks near the edges of a part, each a
  loss of continuity); (c) B as a library option on rectangular meshes only. Taken: (a), the only one defined on any mesh with
  the fewest fallbacks; (b) is listed as a question for the addendum.
- **O3. Fallback or refusal** where the candidates cannot carry a plane: taken the fallback to the linear stencil with the
  list, as `Quadratic` does (ADR 0251 D4), so a case does not stop at one bad cell.
- **O4. The name**: `Continuous`.

## Limits of this registration

- L1. Continuity holds where no candidate changes kind and no fallback occurs. The kind changes of the background next to the
  hole are not touched: D2 of TG_2 over all cells is not expected to reach 4 (ADR 0359 H5, `9.9e-6` at a ratio of 1.85).
- L2. The interpolation error is about 1.2 times that of B and of the library's stencil on a uniform grid (the arithmetic
  above); with cells of aspect `a`, `h_k` is the larger extent and the support spans `1.5 a` cells across the thin direction,
  so the error in units of the small spacing grows about as `a^2` (model: 0.75 at `a = 2`, 1.69 at `a = 3`, against 0.195).
- L3. Exact for linear fields only (not quadratic; `Quadratic` stays a separate option).
- L4. The checks are 2-D; the 3-D behaviour is the model arithmetic only.
- L5. The candidate bins cost memory of about the number of cells times the bins a support box overlaps (16 in 2-D at unit
  aspect).
