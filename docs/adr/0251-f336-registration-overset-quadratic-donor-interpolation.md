Clean-room. The method is derived in this text from first principles (Taylor's theorem, a constrained minimum, the QR
factorisation). The names of the ideas, cited from general knowledge (no paper, book, code or vendor document was opened
for this work): moving least squares, whose weights are the minimum of a weighted norm under polynomial reproduction
constraints (P. Lancaster and K. Salkauskas, "Surfaces generated by moving least squares methods", Math. Comp. 37 (1981)
141-158; the dual form is in D. Levin, "The approximation power of moving least-squares", Math. Comp. 67 (1998)

Barth and P. O. Frederickson, AIAA paper 90-0013, 1990; C. Ollivier-Gooch and M. Van Altena, J. Comput. Phys. 181 (2002)
729-752); Householder QR with column pivoting and its use for least-squares problems (G. H. Golub and C. F. Van Loan,
"Matrix Computations", Johns Hopkins; the role of row ordering for rows of very different size: A. J. Cox and N. J.
Higham, "Stability of Householder QR factorization for weighted least squares problems", 1998); the Lebesgue constant of
an interpolation as the bound of its amplification. The formulas used are derived under "Design"; the citations only name
where such schemes are discussed, and I cite them from memory.

## What F-336 asks

The roadmap entry (acceptance, quoted, binding): "An opt-in donor interpolation that reproduces quadratic fields; with it
the largest error over the calculated and fringe cells of TG_L converges at order 1.7 or more on levels 1 to 3 with dt
proportional to h^2, the root mean square of the error of the calculated component cells of RL of ADR 0232 converges at
order 1.7 or more on levels 1 to 4 (ADR 0246 TQ: 1.922, 1.935, 1.950 with test-supplied stencils), the uniform-flow check
U1 of ADR 0219 holds, and the default stencils and results are bit-identical."

**Reading (registered here).**

1. "Reproduces quadratic fields": every polynomial of total degree two in the solved directions (six monomials in 2-D, ten
   in 3-D) is interpolated exactly up to round-off, on any donor mesh, not only a Cartesian one (checks P2, P3, P4).
2. "With it" is read as: with the opt-in and nothing else; the other settings are the defaults of the registered set-ups
   (the settings PS, implicit Euler, one outer iteration, conservative mode). The opt-ins of ADR 0238 and ADR 0241 are not
   switched on in the acceptance runs.
3. TG_L: the set-up of ADR 0219 as run by ADR 0232 (`200 L^2` steps to `t = 0.5`), the largest `eps_c` over the non-hole
   cells at `t = 0.5`, the two orders between levels 1 and 2 and between 2 and 3 (check A1).
4. RL: the set-up of ADR 0232 amendment 3 as run by ADR 0246 (`200 L` steps), the root mean square of `eps_c` over the
   calculated component cells weighted with the cell volumes, the three orders between successive levels (check A2).
5. U1: the set-up U of ADR 0219 with the opt-in, its limits unchanged (check A3).
6. "The default stencils and results are bit-identical": without the opt-in the stencils are those of M4 bit for bit
   (check D1), Q0 of ADR 0219 and the records R2 and R2' of ADR 0238 hold bit for bit (D2), and the existing overset suites
   pass unedited (D3).

What the code read shows, relevant to the design:

1. The solver uses a stencil only as a list of cells and weights: in the rows of the fringe cells of the momentum and the
   pressure systems (M5, M7), in the interpolated `rAU` and `HbyA` (M6), in the fringe velocity after every corrector (M8)
   and in the refresh M9. Nothing in `rcfd_fv::overset` depends on the size of a stencil or on the sign of a weight, with
   one exception (item 2). ADR 0246 ran the solver with nine-cell stencils put in by the test after `prepare`.
2. `rAU` of a fringe cell is the interpolant of the `rAU` of its stencil cells. With weights that are not all positive it
   is no longer a convex combination, so on a donor mesh whose `rAU` varies strongly over a stencil it could become zero or
   negative; the pressure matrix would lose its sign. The default weights are almost positive (smallest `-0.036`).
3. The kinds of all cells are known before the first stencil is built, so a stencil can ask of any cell whether it is
   calculated.
4. `Overset` holds the face neighbours of every cell; there is no table of the cells around a point.
5. `Connectivity` is built by a struct literal in two places, both in `rcfd-mesh`; `OversetInfo` in one place, in
   `rcfd-fv`. No other crate uses the overset types (the case-tree wiring of F-134 is not delivered).

## Arithmetic before this registration (disclosed, by hand)

- Monomials of total degree at most two: `1 + d + d (d + 1) / 2`, that is 3, 6, 10 for `d = 1, 2, 3` solved directions.
- Cells that share a point with a cell (the cell itself included): 9 on a quadrilateral mesh that is one cell thick, 27
  on a hexahedral mesh; on triangles about 13, on tetrahedra several tens (about 70 on a regular filling). The donor and
  its face neighbours: 5 and 7.
- The nine-cell stencil of ADR 0246 at the corner of a donor cell (`xi = eta = 1/2`): the one-dimensional weights are
  `(-1/8, 3/4, 3/8)`, sum of the absolute values `1.25`; the product has `1.5625` in 2-D and `1.953` in 3-D. Its smallest
  weight there is `-3/32 = -0.094`; ADR 0246 K2 printed `-0.1215` over all offsets.
- Truncation of an interpolation that is exact for quadratics, at a point `x` with stencil points `x_k`, for a field with
  third derivatives bounded by `M_3`: `abs(sum_k w_k f(x_k) - f(x)) <= (sum_k abs(w_k)) max_k abs(x_k - x)^3 M_3 / 6`
  (Taylor's theorem with the remainder of third order; the terms up to the second order cancel by the constraints). With
  the largest distance `1.5 sqrt(2) h` of a nine-cell block from a corner point and a sum of `1.6`: `<= 2.6 h^3 M_3`, of
  third order. ADR 0246 K2 measured `2.72e-5`, `3.40e-6`, `4.24e-7` at levels 1, 2, 4 with the product weights.
- Whether the neighbourhoods are whole in the registered set-ups (a "whole" neighbourhood has all its cells calculated):
  - RL: the nine cells of ADR 0246 are the cells that share a point with the donor; K2 found all of them calculated at
    levels 1, 2, 4.
  - TG_L at level 1 (the tightest): a background fringe centre lies at most `0.25 + sqrt(2) H = 0.361` from the component
    centre in the maximum norm of the component's frame, its donor centre at most `0.03` further, the donor's point
    neighbours `0.06` further: `0.451`, inside the component fringe ring that begins at `0.54`. A component fringe centre
    lies at `0.57`; its donor centre and that one's point neighbours reach inwards by at most `1.5 sqrt(2) H = 0.167`:
    `0.403`, outside the background fringe (at most `0.361`). So every neighbourhood is whole.
  - U (background `h = 0.1`, component cell `0.075`, hole `0.25`): a component fringe centre at `0.5625`, its
    neighbourhood reaches in to `0.5625 - 0.212 = 0.35`, and the background fringe reaches out to `0.25 + 0.141 = 0.39`.
    Some neighbourhoods will lack one or two cells at some poses (not a whole row: the donor centre is at least `0.49`
    out). A background fringe centre at most `0.39`, its donor's neighbours at most `0.525`, where the component fringe
    begins: whole.
  - TR (the cylinder): the background fringe lies near `r = 1.0` to `1.15` in an O-grid from `0.5` to `1.63`, the component
    fringe at `r = 1.6` over background cells of `0.1`: whole by the same estimate (`1.6 - 0.26 = 1.34 > 1.15`).
- A donor cell with a face on a wall, or next to the fringe ring of its own part, has no row of cells on that side: its
  neighbourhood is a block of `3 x 2` cells in 2-D, on which `1, y, y^2` across the two rows are linearly dependent. A
  quadratic cannot be fitted there; this is the case the fallback is for.
- Expectation for RL from ADR 0246: with an interpolation of third order the error of the calculated component cells is the
  error of the background alone, `3.24e-4`, `8.55e-5`, `3.90e-5`, `2.23e-5` (orders `1.922`, `1.935`, `1.950`); with the
  linear stencils it is `1.549e-4`, `6.39e-5`, `3.98e-5`, `2.49e-5`.
- Expectation for TG_L from ADR 0232: the largest error with the linear stencils is `1.0785e-3`, `3.514e-4`, `1.712e-4`, in
  a component fringe cell, of which the truncation of the interpolation is `1.05`, `0.84`, `0.81`. Without that truncation
  the largest error should be near the largest error of the calculated cells of that run, `8.73e-4`, `2.10e-4`, `1.10e-4`
  (orders `2.06`, `1.58`), or near that of the calculated background cells, `7.68e-4`, `2.05e-4`, `1.00e-4` (`1.91`,
  `1.77`). The second pair of levels is close to the limit `1.7` in both.

## Design

### D1. The opt-in

`rcfd_mesh::overset` gets

- `enum DonorInterpolation { Linear, Quadratic }`, default `Linear` (M4, unchanged);
- `Overset::set_donor_interpolation(DonorInterpolation)` and `Overset::donor_interpolation()`. An `Overset` built by
  `Overset::new` is `Linear`. `OversetSolver` takes its stencils from `coupling.overset.connectivity`, so a solver built
  on a coupling whose `Overset` was switched to `Quadratic` uses the new stencils in every step and in its construction;
  nothing else of the solver changes its behaviour;
- `quadratic_weights(centres, x, dims)`, public like `linear_weights` (D3);
- two new fields of `Connectivity`: `interpolation` (the mode it was built with) and `fallbacks` (the fringe cells whose
  stencil is the linear one although `Quadratic` was asked for, in increasing order; empty with `Linear`), with
  `n_fallback()`.

`rcfd_fv::overset` gets a field `n_fallback` of `OversetInfo` (the number of fallbacks of the step) and the refusal of D5.
`linear_weights` and the `Linear` branch of `connectivity` are not touched.

### D2. The stencil: which cells

For a fringe cell with the donor cell `d` (found as in M3, unchanged: the cell of the other part that contains the fringe
centre; "no donor" when there is none or when `d` is not calculated): the **point neighbourhood** of `d`, that is `d` and
every cell that shares at least one mesh point with `d`, of which only the **calculated** cells are kept. `d` is the first
cell of the stencil, the others follow in increasing cell number. A fringe cell and a hole cell are never in a stencil
(their values are interpolated or fixed, not solved; a fringe row that referred to another fringe row would also couple
the two interpolations). The two parts share no point, so every cell of the neighbourhood belongs to the donor's part.

Why this neighbourhood and not another (question taken below, S1):

- it is defined by the topology of any mesh the library can hold (polyhedral cells with any number of faces), not by an
  index structure;
- on a quadrilateral or hexahedral part it is the block of 9 or 27 cells, which reaches one cell beyond the donor in every
  direction. The alternative that also works on any mesh, the face neighbours of the face neighbours, reaches two cells
  along the axes (13 cells in 2-D), and the width of the overlap is what an overset set-up has least of;
- it contains at least as many cells as a quadratic needs on every usual cell type (9, 27, about 13, several tens against
  6, 6, 10, 10), with spare cells that make the fit a least-squares one.

The table of the cells around every point is built once, when `Quadratic` is first set (one pass over the faces of the
cells), and never for `Linear`.

### D3. The weights

Notation: `x` the fringe centre (for a background fringe cell mapped into the component's reference frame, as in M4),
`x_k`, `k = 1..n`, the centres of the stencil cells, `r_k = x_k - x` in the `d` solved directions, `h^2 = (1/n) sum_k
abs(r_k)^2`, `s_k = r_k / h`, and `a_k` the vector of the `m = 1 + d + d (d + 1) / 2` monomials of total degree at most two
of `s_k` (`1`; `s_i`; `s_i s_j` for `i <= j`). `e_0 = (1, 0, ..., 0)` is that vector at `s = 0`.

A set of weights reproduces every quadratic exactly if and only if `sum_k w_k a_k = e_0` (a quadratic `q` is `c . a(s)`,
so `sum_k w_k q(x_k) = c . sum_k w_k a_k = c . e_0 = q(x)`). The first of these `m` equations is `sum_k w_k = 1`. For `n
> m` the equations leave freedom, which is used as in M4: the weights are the minimum of

`J(w) = sum_k w_k^2 / omega_k`,  `omega_k = 1 / (abs(s_k)^2 + 1e-6)`,

under `sum_k w_k a_k = e_0`. `omega_k` is the weight of M4 (the same expression and the same `1e-6`): it favours the cells
near the point, and a point that coincides with a cell centre gets that cell's value up to about `1e-6`. With the Lagrange
multipliers `lambda`, `w_k = omega_k a_k . lambda` and `(sum_k omega_k a_k a_k^T) lambda = e_0`: these are the weights with
which the weighted least-squares fit of a quadratic to the values `f_k` (the minimum of `sum_k omega_k (f_k - c . a_k)^2`
over `c`) gives its value at `x`, `c_0 = sum_k w_k f_k`. So the interpolation is the value at the fringe centre of the
weighted least-squares quadratic through the calculated cells of the donor's point neighbourhood; M4 is the same
construction with the monomials of degree at most one on the face neighbours.

Solution. The normal matrix above has the square of the condition number of the fit, and the `omega_k` can differ by a
factor `1e6`, so it is not formed. With `B` the `n x m` matrix of the rows `sqrt(omega_k) a_k^T` and `v_k = w_k /
sqrt(omega_k)`, the problem is the minimum of `abs(v)^2` under `B^T v = e_0`. A Householder QR factorisation with column
pivoting, `B P = Q R`, gives `v = Q_1 y` with `R^T y = P^T e_0` (`Q_1` the first `m` columns of `Q`; `v` lies in the range
of `B`, which is where the minimum of the norm lies). The rows of `B` are taken in the order of decreasing `omega_k` (the
rows of very different size first, as the analysis of weighted problems asks); the weights are returned in the order of
the cells.

`quadratic_weights` fails ("refused") when

- (R1) `n < m`: fewer than 6 cells in 2-D, 10 in 3-D (3 with one solved direction);
- (R2) the fit is rank deficient: the smallest `abs(R_ii)` is at most `1e-10` of the largest. This is the `3 x 2` block of
  the arithmetic above, a row of cells in a line, coincident centres;
- (R3) `sum_k abs(w_k) > 4`, or a weight is not finite. The sum is the Lebesgue constant of the stencil: an error of size
  `delta` in the donor values changes the interpolant by at most `sum abs(w) delta`, and the truncation bound of the
  arithmetic above carries the same factor. A whole block has about `1.6` (2-D) to `2` (3-D); `4` is twice that. A
  neighbourhood that lies mostly on one side of the point (an extrapolation) is refused by this number before its
  weights grow large.

### D4. The fallback, and how it is seen

A fringe cell whose quadratic stencil is refused (R1, R2 or R3) gets **the linear stencil of M4** (the donor and its
calculated face neighbours with `linear_weights`), bit for bit the stencil it has without the opt-in, and its cell number
is recorded in `Connectivity::fallbacks`. `OversetInfo::n_fallback` reports the count of every step. A fallback is
therefore a local loss of one order of the interpolation at that cell, never a silent change of the others and never a
failure of the run; if M4 fails too, the error is the "no donor" error of today. Nothing is extrapolated and no wider
neighbourhood is searched (question S4).

### D5. What the weights are, and what follows

- **They sum to one** (the first constraint), so a uniform field stays uniform: U1 holds by construction.
- **They reproduce linear and quadratic fields**, in the reference frame of the donor's part; a rigid motion maps
  quadratics to quadratics, so also in the frame of the fringe cell.
- **They can be negative** (the product weights of ADR 0246 reach `-0.12`). The interpolant is then not a convex
  combination: it can lie outside the range of the donor values, by at most `(sum abs(w) - 1) / 2` times the spread of
  the values over the stencil. **Nothing is clipped**: a clipped or limited weight set would lose the exactness for
  quadratics (and, if renormalised differently for different quantities, the consistency of `u`, `HbyA` and `rAU`), and
  the overset path transports no bounded scalar: the interpolated quantities are `u`, `p`, `HbyA` and `rAU`. For `u`, `p`
  and `HbyA` an overshoot of third order is harmless.
- **`rAU` must stay positive.** With `Quadratic` the solver checks every interpolated `rAU` of a fringe cell and refuses
  the step by name ("overset: the interpolated rAU of the fringe cell ... is not positive") when one is not. This is a
  visible refusal instead of a clip. The check is made only when the connectivity was built with `Quadratic` (or says so),
  so that the default path is unchanged in every respect.
- **The fringe rows are not diagonally dominant** when `sum abs(w) > 1` (also true of M4, by `0.07`). The linear solvers of
  the overset path (BiCGStab with ILU(0), GMRES with AMG) coped with the nine-cell rows in ADR 0246; the count of
  unconverged solves is printed for every run of this registration.
- **The interpolation is not conservative**, as M4 is not; the mass offset `delta` of M7 is unchanged and keeps the mass of
  the domain exact.
- **The weights are continuous in the pose** while the set of stencil cells stays the same, and jump when the donor or a
  kind changes, as those of M4 do.

### D6. Cost

Per fringe cell and pose: a QR factorisation of an `n x m` matrix (`9 x 6`: a few hundred operations; `27 x 10`: a few
thousand) against the `3 x 3` or `4 x 4` system of M4. The rows of the fringe cells in the momentum and pressure matrices
have `n + 1` entries instead of `6` (2-D) or `8` (3-D). The fringe is a set of codimension one, so both costs are small
against the flow solve; they are measured and printed (the time of `connectivity` in both modes at RL level 4, the run
times of the flow runs against those of ADR 0246), without a limit, because the machine is shared and timings on it are
not reproducible to better than a factor of two.

### Questions settled by taking the recommendation

Each is taken on the maintainer's standing instruction of 2026-11-11 (take the recommendation); none is a decision the
maintainer made in person.

- **S1. Neighbourhood**: (a) the point neighbourhood; (b) the face neighbours of the face neighbours; (c) grow until enough
  cells are found. Recommendation (a), for the reasons under D2.
- **S2. Weighting**: (a) the `omega_k` of M4; (b) another kernel. Recommendation (a): one family of stencils, the same

- **S3. Clipping**: (a) none, a refusal for a non-positive `rAU`; (b) clip. Recommendation (a).
- **S4. Fallback**: (a) the linear stencil, reported; (b) an error; (c) a wider or one-sided search. Recommendation (a);
  (c) is listed as a possible follow-up in the addendum if the fallback count is not zero where it matters.
- **S5. Where the switch lives**: (a) on `Overset` (the stencils are its product); (b) on `OversetSolver`. Recommendation
  (a), with the count in `OversetInfo`.

Not taken, because it would change a default: making `Quadratic` the default (ADR 0246 question Q3). Listed as open in the
addendum.

### Classes

merged. A control (CM, NL, NK) that does not hold, or a mutation that does not make its check fail, is a defective set-up:
corrected by an amendment before any re-run, never counted as a pass.

## Decision rule

- **done**: A1, A2, A3, D1, D2 and D3 are met, P1 to P10, C1, C2 and E1 are met, the controls held and the mutations failed
  their checks.
- **partial**: the opt-in exists, reproduces quadratics (P1 to P5 met) and leaves the defaults bit-identical (D1 to D3 met),
  A3 is met, but an order of A1 or A2 misses its limit with its cause diagnosed, or another P or F check is missed, or a
  control or mutation did not hold and was not corrected.
- **todo**: the interpolation does not reproduce quadratics, or D1 to D3 cannot be met (the change is then not merged), or
  no registered measurement could be run.

## Limits of this registration

- Flow checks on Taylor-Green and uniform flow only, in 2-D, on uniform Cartesian parts; the cylinder case TR is used for
  the stencils (P) and without the opt-in (R2), not for a flow run with Q. The 3-D check is kinematic.
- A donor next to a wall or next to the fringe ring of its own part falls back to the linear stencil (D4); a set-up with a
  thin overlap gets little from the opt-in. The count says so, the run does not fail.
- The order checks A1 and A2 are those of the acceptance: a pointwise maximum at one pose on three levels and a root mean
  square on four. They share the weaknesses that ADR 0232 and ADR 0246 found in such measures.
- The mechanism by which the response to the truncation of the linear interpolation stays below second order (F-349) is
  not touched: this item removes the truncation, it does not explain the response.
- No clipping: the interpolation is not bounded. It is not meant for a bounded transported scalar, which the overset path
  does not have.
- The cost is measured on a shared machine and reported without a limit.
