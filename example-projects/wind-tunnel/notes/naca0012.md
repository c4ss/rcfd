# NACA 0012 aerofoil: k-omega SST at 4 and 2 degrees

## What it is

The symmetric NACA 0012 aerofoil (chord c = 1 m) in a uniform stream U = 1 m/s at chord Reynolds number 6e6, the
incompressible condition of the repository's NACA 0012 registry set-up (ADR 0092, Ladson's condition), with Menter's
k-omega SST turbulence model. Lift and drag are reported as coefficients.

* `cases/naca0012-aoa4.toml`: angle of attack 4 degrees. `cases/naca0012-aoa2.toml`: the variant at 2 degrees. The angle
  is set four times in each case (initial velocity, far-field velocity, lift direction, drag direction); the staleness test
  checks that the four agree.
* Mesh: `meshes/naca0012-ogrid`, an OpenFOAM polyMesh folder built by `scripts/make_example_projects.py` from
  `meshes/naca0012-ogrid.recipe.toml`: the repository's cylinder O-grid (128 cells around, 48 radial) with its points
  moved onto the NACA 0012 surface and a far-field circle of 20 chords, exactly as ADR 0092's set-up does (6144 cells).
* Solver: SIMPLEC, second-order upwind convection, 2000 iterations. Stored: `ux`, `uy`, `p`, `nut` every 200 iterations
  (10 snapshots), single precision. Reports: `cl`, `cd` (reference area c x depth), every iteration.
* `cases/naca0012-aoa4-rotated.toml` and `cases/naca0012-aoa2-rotated.toml`: the same two angles set by rotating the mesh
  instead of the flow (ADR 0155), every other setting the same; see the next section.

## How to set the angle of attack: rotate the flow, or rotate the mesh

Both ways describe the same flow; pick the one that reads better for your case.

1. **Rotate the flow** (`cases/naca0012-aoa4.toml`): the mesh stays as it is (chord along +x) and the angle appears in four
   settings that must agree: `initial.velocity` and `boundaries.farfield.velocity_value` = `(cos a, sin a, 0)`,
   `reports.cl.direction` = `(-sin a, cos a, 0)` (lift, normal to the stream) and `reports.cd.direction` =
   `(cos a, sin a, 0)` (drag, along the stream).
2. **Rotate the mesh** (`cases/naca0012-aoa4-rotated.toml`): the flow stays along +x, lift along +y, drag along +x, and one
   table turns the aerofoil:

   ```toml
   [mesh.transform]
   rotate_z_deg = -4.0                # angle of attack 4 degrees
   rotate_origin = [0.25, 0.0, 0.0]   # the quarter chord
   ```

   Sign convention: a positive `rotate_z_deg` turns the mesh counter-clockwise seen from +z (x to the right, y up). An
   angle of attack lifts the nose into the oncoming flow, which is a clockwise turn, so `rotate_z_deg = -alpha`. The
   far-field circle turns with the aerofoil; every other setting (velocities, report directions) stays in world
   coordinates, and `rcfd check` warns (MT101) about settings that place something relative to the geometry, such as a
   probe point or a box, because those do not turn with the mesh. The Mesh side of the Studio shows the rotated aerofoil.

The two ways give the same lift and drag here: `cl` 0.3481949506 (rotated mesh) against 0.3481949567 (rotated flow) at 4
degrees, a relative difference of 1.8e-8, and 9.5e-8 at 2 degrees; `cd` within 1.6e-8 and 5.6e-8 (ADR 0155 E2). The
rotated-mesh problem is the flow-rotated one turned as a whole, far field included, so only round-off and numerics that
depend on the axes (the inner solves of each velocity component) differ; they are largest in the first iterations (`cl`
3e-4 to 7e-4 apart near iteration 20) and fade as the run settles.

## What to click

1. Select the run of `cases/naca0012-aoa4.toml`; the Viewport shows `p`: the suction peak near the leading edge on the
   upper side and the stagnation point below it. Zoom in with the mouse wheel; the far field is 20 chords away.
2. Slide over the iterations: the field settles in the first few hundred iterations; the Plots panel shows `cl` and `cd`
   levelling out and the residuals stalling near 1e-6.
3. Choose `nut` to see the eddy viscosity of the boundary layers and the wake.
4. Select the run of `cases/naca0012-aoa2.toml`: smaller suction peak, smaller `cl`.
5. `designs/variants.ledger.jsonl` holds `cl` and `cd` of both runs; the lift slope of this mesh is their difference over
   2 degrees.
6. Select the run of `cases/naca0012-aoa4-rotated.toml`: the same flow on a mesh turned nose up, the free stream along
   +x; its `cl` and `cd` curves in the Plots panel match those of `cases/naca0012-aoa4.toml` after the first few dozen
   iterations. Select the case itself: the Mesh side shows the rotated aerofoil, and the Case editor shows
   `mesh` > `transform`.

## How it relates to the benchmark, and the limits

The repository's benchmark descriptor `../../cases/naca0012/case.json` names the NACA 0012 for the incompressible lift slope,
indicatively within 5 percent of 0.11 per degree before stall, and notes that an aerofoil mesh generator was missing. This
example uses the coarse O-grid that ADR 0092 built only to check the set-up (`rcfd check`); ADR 0092 never ran it. These
runs are the first solves on it, and they are not a validation:

* The runs stop at the 2000-iteration cap with the residual targets not met (solver state "not_converged", exit code 5);
  `cl` still drifts slowly over the last thousand iterations (at 4 degrees from 0.3471 at iteration 1000 to 0.3482 at
  2000, 0.3 percent) and the momentum residual stops near 1.5e-6.
* The first cell is about 0.01 chord thick, so the wall y+ is about a thousand, beyond the range the SST wall treatment
  is meant for; the trailing-edge cells are highly skewed (maximum non-orthogonality 80 degrees, rule SV101).
  Expect the lift well below the measured slope (about 0.108 per degree in Ladson's tripped data at this Reynolds
  number, as stored and read in `../../docs/reference-data.md`; thin-aerofoil theory 0.1097) and the drag at two to three
  times the measured minimum drag (0.0080 in the same data). The run table of `notes/README.md` gives the numbers.
* At 8 degrees the same set-up did not converge (the lift swung between -0.1 and 0.9 over 2000 iterations; with
  local pseudo-time steps or SIMPLE it diverged, with a limited linear upwind scheme the lift drifted to 0.06), so the
  variant is at 2 degrees (ADR 0142, rule-d smoke). A finer O-grid or a C-grid is needed for higher angles.
* The Case editor of the Studio cannot find the mesh of these two cases (its relative paths resolve against the Studio's
  working folder); runs are not affected. See `notes/README.md`, Limits.
