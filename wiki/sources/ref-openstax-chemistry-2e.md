# OpenStax Chemistry 2e, sec. 12.5 "Collision Theory"

**Origin:** OpenStax, *Chemistry 2e*, sec. 12.5 "Collision Theory", URL above.

## Key points

- Collision theory postulates: the rate is proportional to the rate of reactant collisions; colliding species must be oriented so the atoms that bond in the product touch; the collision must have enough energy.
- Arrhenius equation $k = Ae^{-E_a/(RT)}$: $A$ the frequency factor (collision frequency and orientation), $E_a$ the activation energy (J/mol), $R = 8.314$ J/(mol K), $T$ in kelvin.
- Linear form $\ln k = -\frac{E_a}{R}\frac1T + \ln A$: a plot of $\ln k$ against $1/T$ is a straight line of slope $-E_a/R$; two-temperature form $\ln(k_1/k_2) = \frac{E_a}{R}\left(\frac1{T_2} - \frac1{T_1}\right)$.
- Example 12.13 (HI decomposition): slope about $-2.2\times10^4$ K, $E_a \approx 180$ kJ/mol.
