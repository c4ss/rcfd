## Summary

Entry-level statement of the three modes of heat transfer with the rate laws a first-year reader needs: conduction through a slab, natural convection as buoyancy-driven mass motion, and Stefan-Boltzmann radiation.

## Key points by section

### 14.4 "Heat Transfer Methods"
- Conduction is "heat transfer through stationary matter by physical contact"; convection is "heat transfer by the macroscopic movement of fluid"; radiation is transfer by emitted or absorbed electromagnetic waves.

### 14.5 "Conduction"
- Rate through a slab: $Q/t = kA(T_2 - T_1)/d$, with thermal conductivity $k$ (J/(s m °C)), area $A$, thickness $d$; rate proportional to $A$, $\Delta T$ and $k$, inversely to $d$.
- Mechanism: molecular collisions pass kinetic energy from hotter to cooler regions; no net transfer at equilibrium; metals (good electrical conductors) are generally good heat conductors.
- Table of $k$ near 0 °C: air 0.023, water 0.6, ice 2.2, glass 0.84, stainless steel 14, iron 80, aluminium 220, copper 390, silver 420, Styrofoam 0.010.
- R factor $R = d/k$; conduction rate inversely proportional to $R$.

### 14.6 "Convection"
- Convection is "heat transfer by the macroscopic movement of mass"; it "can be natural or forced". Natural convection is "driven by buoyant forces: hot air rises because density decreases as temperature increases".
- Air "is a poor conductor and thus a good insulator" when its motion is suppressed; the space available for airflow decides whether air acts as an insulator. Worked examples use $Q = mc\Delta T$ and $Q = mL_v$; no numbered convection rate equation is given.

### 14.7 "Radiation"
- Stefan-Boltzmann law $Q/t = \sigma e A T^4$, $\sigma = 5.67\times10^{-8}$ J/(s m$^2$ K$^4$), absolute temperature $T$.
- Emissivity $e$: 1 for an ideal black body, 0 for a perfect reflector; examples: carbon black about 0.99, human skin in the infrared 0.97, Earth average about 0.65, tungsten filament about 0.5.
- Net rate for an object at $T_1$ in surroundings at $T_2$: $Q_{net}/t = \sigma e A(T_2^4 - T_1^4)$.
- Radiation needs no medium; a black body is a perfect absorber and a perfect emitter.
