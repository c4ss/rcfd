## Key points

- **Basic principles:** for an axially symmetric rotor at constant spin speed $\Omega$, $\mathbf M\ddot{\mathbf q} + (\mathbf C + \mathbf G)\dot{\mathbf q} + (\mathbf K + \mathbf N)\mathbf q = \mathbf f(t)$, with symmetric mass $\mathbf M$, damping $\mathbf C$ and bearing / seal stiffness $\mathbf K$, a skew-symmetric gyroscopic matrix $\mathbf G$ proportional to $\Omega$, and $\mathbf N$ for deflection-dependent effects such as centrifugal elements; the eigenvectors are complex and depend on speed; positive cross-coupled stiffness can destabilise the rotor.
- **Campbell diagram:** also "whirl speed map"; whirl frequencies against spin speed; the backward-whirl and forward-whirl curves diverge as speed increases; where a curve crosses the synchronous line (whirl frequency equal to spin speed) the response may peak, a critical speed; the lowest is the first critical speed (lead).
- **Jeffcott rotor:** massless flexible shaft on two bearings with a rigid disc at mid-span, only lateral motion; balanced, it is a single-degree-of-freedom oscillator; an eccentricity $\varepsilon$ gives $m\ddot r + kr = m\varepsilon\Omega^2\sin\Omega t$; "a mathematical idealization".
