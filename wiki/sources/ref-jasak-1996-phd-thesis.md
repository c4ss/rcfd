## Summary

Chapter 3 sets out a second-order, collocated, segregated finite-volume method on arbitrary polyhedral meshes: mesh and face addressing, term-by-term discretisation of a scalar transport equation, time schemes, boundary conditions, a bounded convection scheme (Gamma) built on the normalised-variable approach, the treatment of non-orthogonality, the linear solvers and under-relaxation, the numerical errors of each choice, and the PISO and SIMPLE procedures for incompressible Navier-Stokes. Chapters 4 and 5 propose a-posteriori error estimates and use them to drive local mesh refinement.

## Key points (own summary)

- **Method properties (3.1):** integral form over each control volume, so mass and momentum are conserved discretely; fixed Cartesian frame; arbitrary polyhedral cells; all variables on the same control volumes, "usually called the colocated or non-staggered variable arrangement (Rhie and Chow [117], Peric [109])"; segregated solution with explicit inter-equation coupling and lagged nonlinear terms.
- **Mesh (3.2, Eqn. 3.1):** control volumes do not overlap and fill the domain; the computational point is the centroid; each flat face is shared with one neighbour; the face area vector points out of the "owner" (lower label) into the "neighbour".
- **Second order by linear variation (3.3, Eqns. 3.3-3.7):** assuming `phi` varies linearly in space and time around P, the leading truncation term scales with the square of the cell size (and of `dt`). The integral form (3.8) is discretised term by term with the discrete Gauss theorem `(div a) V_P = sum_f S . a_f` (3.15).
- **Convection (3.3.1.1-3.3.1.2, Eqns. 3.17-3.23):** `sum_f F phi_f` with the face mass flux `F = S . (rho U)_f` (3.18). Central differencing (CD) interpolates linearly with `f_x = |fN| / |PN|` (3.19-3.20); it is second order even on non-uniform meshes but oscillates for convection-dominated problems. Upwind differencing (UD) takes the upstream value (3.21) and is bounded but adds numerical diffusion. Blended differencing mixes the two with a factor `gamma` (3.22-3.23).
- **Diffusion and non-orthogonality (3.3.1.3, Eqns. 3.24-3.34):** on an orthogonal mesh (`d` parallel to `S`) the face-normal gradient comes from the two cell values across the face alone (3.25), which has a smaller truncation error than interpolating cell gradients; the cell gradient by Gauss is `(grad phi)_P = (1/V_P) sum_f S phi_f` (3.26), interpolated to the face (3.27). On non-orthogonal meshes `S = Delta + k` with `Delta` parallel to `d` (3.28-3.29): the orthogonal part is implicit, the correction `k . (grad phi)_f` explicit. Three decompositions: minimum correction (`Delta` and `k` orthogonal, 3.30), orthogonal correction (the contribution of P and N kept as on an orthogonal mesh, 3.31), over-relaxed (the P-N part grows with non-orthogonality, 3.32). The correction "potentially creates unboundedness".
- **Sources (3.3.1.4, Eqns. 3.35-3.36):** linearise `S(phi) = S_u + S_p phi`; treat the source "as implicitly as possible"; put `S_p` into the diagonal only if negative (3.5).
- **Time (3.3.2, Eqns. 3.37-3.55):** Crank-Nicolson, second order and unconditionally stable but not bounded (3.41); the general algebraic form `a_P phi_P + sum_N a_N phi_N = R` and its sparse matrix (3.42-3.43); explicit, with the Courant number limit "If the Courant number is larger than unity, the explicit system becomes unstable" (3.47-3.48); Euler implicit, first order, stable when the Courant limit is violated and bounded (3.49-3.50); backward differencing, second order from three time levels, not guaranteed bounded (3.51-3.55).
- **Boundary conditions (3.3.3):** numerical conditions are fixed value (Dirichlet) and fixed gradient (von Neumann); "radiation boundaries, for example" couple value and gradient; a fixed-gradient face value is `phi_P` plus the distance times the gradient, first order because `d` does not hit the face centre. Physical conditions for incompressible flow: inlet (velocity fixed, pressure zero gradient), outlet (either velocities projected and scaled with pressure zero gradient, unstable under inflow, or fixed pressure with velocity zero gradient), symmetry plane (normal gradient zero), no-slip wall (velocity fixed, pressure zero gradient).
- **TVD and NVD (3.4.1):** flux limiting gives schemes above first order without the oscillations of classical second-order schemes; Harten's TVD; Sweby writes a limited scheme as UD plus a limited higher-order correction with limiter `psi(r)` of consecutive gradients (3.65-3.69) and gives the TVD region (Sweby's diagram); the TVD criterion depends on the Courant number, which "cannot be accepted" for steady solutions; TVD schemes are "either too diffusive" (MINMOD) "or too compressive" (SUPERBEE). The normalised variable of Leonard (built from the upwind, central and downwind values around the face, 3.70) and Gaskell and Lau's convection boundedness criterion (CBC) define a bounded region in the NVD; "the only basic differencing scheme that satisfies the boundedness criterion is UD" (Fig. 3.11, which shows UD, CD, LUD of Warming and Beam and QUICK).
- **Convergence of limited schemes (3.4.1.3):** any bounded scheme above first order must be nonlinear; NVD schemes may not give a unique steady solution; TVD convergence is proven only for explicit calculations.
- **NVD on unstructured meshes and Gamma (3.4.2-3.4.3):** the far-upwind value is replaced by an expression in the cell gradient and the gradient across the face, so no far-upwind node or mesh topology is needed (the OCR of the formula, Eqns. 3.79-3.86, is not legible); the Gamma scheme blends UD and CD smoothly for `0 < phi~_C < beta_m`, with `beta_m` "ideally ... about 0.1" for sharp profiles and larger values as "A universal remedy for convergence problems", and its face value does not depend on the Courant number.
- **Matrix quality, deferred correction, relaxation (3.5, Eqns. 3.94-3.96):** iterative solvers need diagonal dominance; only UD gives a diagonally equal convection matrix; Khosla and Rubin's deferred correction puts UD in the matrix and the rest in the source, which improves the matrix but not boundedness; the non-orthogonal correction goes to the source and is updated by repeated solutions ("non-orthogonal correctors"); in steady state, under-relaxation adds an artificial term to both sides with the previous-iteration value and factor `0 < alpha <= 1` (3.96), cancelling at convergence. Solvers: ICCG for symmetric, Bi-CGSTAB for asymmetric matrices.
- **Numerical errors (3.6):** numerical diffusion of convection schemes and of time schemes; mesh-induced errors: resolution, non-orthogonality error `E_d` when the correction is discarded (smallest for minimum correction), skewness error when the P-N line misses the face centre by `m`, reducing face integrals to first order (3.129).
- **Non-orthogonality results (3.7.4):** on a Laplace test the minimum correction approach diverges at 40 deg, the orthogonal correction stops converging at 45 deg, the over-relaxed approach still converges at 65 deg with many correctors; conclusion (3.9): the over-relaxed approach has the best stability and convergence "although it causes the largest discretisation error if the nonorthogonal correction is discarded".
- **Pressure equation (3.8.1, Eqns. 3.136-3.144):** semi-discrete momentum `a_P U_P = H(U) - grad p` (3.136), "In the spirit of the Rhie and Chow procedure", with the pressure gradient not yet discretised; `H(U)` holds the neighbour coefficients times neighbour velocities plus the source part (3.137); `U_P = H(U)/a_P - grad p / a_P` (3.139) interpolated to the faces (3.140); continuity gives `div((1/a_P) grad p) = div(H(U)/a_P)` (3.141); face flux `F = S . (H(U)/a_P)_f - S . (1/a_P)_f (grad p)_f` (3.144), conservative when (3.141) is satisfied.
- **Coupling algorithms (3.8.2):** simultaneous algorithms (Caretto et al., Vanka) solve the whole system at once, at great cost in operations and memory; segregated algorithms (PISO, SIMPLE) solve in sequence.
- **PISO (3.8.2.1):** momentum predictor with the old pressure; pressure solution with `H(U)` from the predicted velocity; explicit velocity correction (3.139), which neglects the neighbour-velocity corrections, so `H(U)` is corrected and the pressure equation repeated; `H(U)` coefficients stay fixed through the correction sequence.
- **SIMPLE (3.8.2.2):** momentum under-relaxed implicitly; pressure under-relaxed explicitly, `p_new = p_old + alpha_p (p_P - p_old)`; recommended values (Peric) `alpha_p = 0.2`, `alpha_u = 0.8`.
- **Error indicators and estimates (4.1, 4.1.1, 4.2, 4.3.1, 4.5):** error indicators are "Combinations of gradients of flow variables", cheap, mark where resolution is needed but give no absolute error level; error estimates should give level and distribution, be cheap, cell by cell, asymptotically exact, modestly over-estimating; Richardson extrapolation needs two meshes; the residual error estimate measures how well each cell satisfies the original equation.
- **Selecting cells (5.2):** with the error scaling as `h^2`, a desired local size follows from a target error; alternatively compare the cell error with the domain mean using refinement and unrefinement factors; refine preferentially normal to the solution gradient.
