## Summary

The first-year picture of stress, strain and elastic moduli (sec. 12.3) and of viscosity, laminar versus turbulent flow, Poiseuille's law and the pipe Reynolds number (sec. 14.7).

## Key points by section

### Sec. 12.3 "Stress, Strain, and Elastic Modulus"
- Stress is force per unit area, in pascals; kinds: tensile, compressive, bulk, shear. Strain is the dimensionless fractional change of length, volume or shape.
- Eq. 12.33: in the linear (low-stress) range, stress = elastic modulus x strain; the modulus has the unit of stress.
- Eq. 12.34-12.36: tensile stress $F_\perp / A$, tensile strain $\Delta L / L_0$, Young's modulus $Y = (F_\perp/A)/(\Delta L/L_0)$.
- Eq. 12.37-12.40: pressure $p = F_\perp / A$ (a scalar acting equally in all directions); bulk strain $\Delta V/V_0$; bulk modulus $B = -\Delta p/(\Delta V/V_0)$; compressibility $k = 1/B$.
- Eq. 12.41-12.43: shear strain $\Delta x / L_0$, shear stress $F_\parallel / A$ (force parallel to the surface), shear modulus $S = (F_\parallel/A)/(\Delta x/L_0)$. The section says these shear moduli apply to solids, not fluids.

### Sec. 14.7 "Viscosity and Turbulence"
- Viscosity is a fluid's internal friction; it is defined on laminar flow, where layers slide without mixing.
- Eq. 14.17: the force to drag a plate at constant speed over a fluid layer is $F = \eta v A / L$, so $\eta = F L / (v A)$; SI unit Pa s.
- Table 14.4 (units $10^{-3}$ Pa s): air 0.0171 (0 C), 0.0181 (20 C), 0.0190 (40 C), 0.0218 (100 C); liquid water 1.792 (0 C), 1.002 (20 C), 0.6947 (37 C), 0.653 (40 C), 0.282 (100 C). Gases are far less viscous than liquids; viscosity depends on temperature.
- Eq. 14.18-14.19 (Poiseuille): $R = 8 \eta l / (\pi r^4)$, $Q = (p_2 - p_1)\, \pi r^4 / (8 \eta l)$; $p_2 - p_1 = R Q$ holds for laminar and turbulent flow.
- Turbulence is caused by obstructions or sharp corners and by high speeds; layers mix through eddies and swirls.
- Eq. 14.20: pipe Reynolds number $N_R = 2 \rho v r / \eta$ (dimensionless). Below about 2000 laminar, above about 3000 turbulent, between about 2000 and 3000 unstable, switching with small changes such as surface roughness.
